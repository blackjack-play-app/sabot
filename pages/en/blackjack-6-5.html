6:5 vs 3:2 blackjack: the costly difference

One small line on the felt, "BLACKJACK PAYS 6 TO 5", and the house edge nearly quadruples. Here is what it really means.

The difference

Blackjack on a $10 betWin
Paid 3:2$15
Paid 6:5$12
Paid 1:1 (some variants)$10

You get a blackjack about once every 21 hands (4.75%). Losing $3 each time adds about 1.4% to the house edge: from about 0.5% to about 1.9%.

What it does to a night out

$10 bets, about 80 hands an hour, perfect basic strategy:

3:2 table6:5 table
Average loss per hourabout $4about $15
Over 4 hoursabout $16about $60

These are long-run averages: a real night can end up or down. But over time the 6:5 table costs almost four times more, even if you play perfectly.

How to spot a 6:5 table

  • It's printed on the felt: "Blackjack pays 6 to 5" instead of "3 to 2".
  • Often low minimums, or a single deck sold as a perk (it doesn't make up for 6:5).
  • Online, check the "Blackjack pays" rule in the table's help.

FAQ

Is single-deck 6:5 a good deal?

No. One deck helps the player by about 0.5%, but 6:5 takes away about 1.4%. Still clearly negative.

Can you count cards at 6:5?

In practice no: 6:5 takes away more than counting can win under normal conditions.

Does 1:1 blackjack exist?

Yes, in some variants (usually with other, friendlier rules). Compare the full rule set.
