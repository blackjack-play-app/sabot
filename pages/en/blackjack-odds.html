This table explains all of basic strategy: against 2 to 6 the dealer busts often, so you stand on 12 to 16 and double more. Against 7 to ace he usually ends on 17 or more: a 16 only wins if he busts, so you hit.

16 against a 10: if you stand, you only win when the dealer busts (23%). If you hit, you bust 62% of the time, but when you don't you often have a real hand. Both lose; hitting (or surrendering if allowed) loses slightly less.

The chance of a blackjack

An ace plus a 10-value card on your first two cards: about 4.75%, roughly one hand in 21. The dealer has the same chance. Paid 3 to 2, blackjack is a big part of what the player wins back; paid 6 to 5, the house edge goes up by about 1.4 points. Avoid 6:5 tables.

The insurance math

The dealer shows an ace and offers insurance: you bet half your stake, paid 2 to 1 if he has a 10 underneath. In a 6-deck shoe, about 96 of the remaining 311 cards are 10s: roughly 31%. Insurance would need more than 33.3% to break even.

Result: insurance loses about 7% of the amount insured. Taking "even money" on a blackjack is the same bet. Only a card counter takes it, from a Hi-Lo true count of +3 (see card counting).

FAQ

What are the odds of a blackjack?

About 4.75%, roughly one hand in 21 with 6 decks.

Which dealer card busts the most?

The 5 and 6 (about 42%), then the 4 (39%). With an ace, the dealer busts only about 17% of the time.

How often does the dealer bust overall?

About 28% of the time across all up cards. The player busts more often because he acts first: that is where the house edge comes from.

Do cards already dealt change the odds?

Yes, a little: that is the idea behind card counting. Many small cards gone = a shoe rich in 10s and aces, better for the player.
