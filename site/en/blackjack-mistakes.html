The 10 most common blackjack mistakes

Most players give the casino 2 to 4 times more than they need to. Here are the mistakes that cost the most, and the myths that keep them going.

  2. Myths
  3. How to fix them

The 10 mistakes

  1. Mimicking the dealer (hit to 17, never double or split). It feels safe, but the house edge climbs to about 5.5%, ten times more than with basic strategy.
  2. Taking insurance "to protect" a good hand. It loses about 7% of the amount insured, whatever your hand. The math.
  3. Standing on 12 to 16 against 7 or higher for fear of busting. The dealer usually ends on 17 or more: standing loses about 3 times in 4.
  4. Hitting 12 to 16 against 4, 5 or 6. The dealer is the one in danger: let him bust (more than 4 times in 10 with a 5 or 6).
  5. Not doubling 10 or 11 against a weak card. These are the most profitable plays in the game. When to double.
  6. Standing on A,7 against 9, 10 or ace. Soft 18 looks good, but you should hit: one card can't bust you.
  7. Splitting 10s. You break a hand that wins nearly 8 times in 10 against a 5 or 6.
  8. Not splitting 8s against a 9 or 10. 16 is the worst hand; two hands of 8 lose less.
  9. Playing at a 6:5 table. Blackjack paid 6 to 5 costs more than all your playing mistakes combined (+1.4% house edge).
  10. Playing fast and tired. Most mistakes happen on hands the player "knows". A few minutes of drills a day is enough to wipe them out.

The myths

  • "The dealer is on a streak, he has to lose." Each round is almost independent of the last. Only card counting measures a real change.
  • "The player at third base took my card." His choices change your cards unpredictably: he helps you as often as he hurts you.
  • "Double your bet after a loss" (Martingale). No betting system changes the house edge. You swap many small wins for a few very big losses.
  • "Counting cards is illegal." It is just thinking. Casinos do have the right to refuse a player, though.
  • "You need to memorise thousands of cards to count." No: Hi-Lo is +1, 0 or -1 per card. How it works.

FAQ

Does a bad player at the table make others lose?

No. His choices change the cards you get, but unpredictably: he helps you as often as he hurts you.

Should you raise your bet after a loss?

No. Betting systems don't change the house edge. Only card counting justifies changing your bet, based on the count, never on past results.

What is the most expensive mistake?

Playing 6:5 and mimicking the dealer. Among playing decisions, not doubling 10 and 11 and taking insurance cost the most.
