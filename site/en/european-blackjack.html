European vs American blackjack: what changes

In most European casinos the dealer has no hole card. That detail changes a handful of basic strategy plays.

On this page

  1. The difference
  2. The effect
  3. Plays that change
  4. FAQ

The key difference: the hole card

American (hole card)European (no hole card)
Dealer's starting cardsone up, one downone, face up
Blackjack checkright away, with an ace or 10after the players act
If he makes blackjackyou only lose your original betyou also lose doubled and split bets
Surrenderoften allowed (late)rare

Some European casinos use "original bets only" (OBO): you only lose your first bet to a dealer blackjack. Then the strategy is the same as American blackjack.

The effect on the house edge

With no hole card, the house edge goes up by about 0.1% with otherwise equal rules. That's small, as long as you adjust the few plays below. Other rules (number of decks, H17 or S17, 3:2 or 6:5) often matter more.

Plays that change

Against a dealer 10 or ace, avoid putting more money on the table:

  • 11 vs 10 or ace: hit (instead of doubling against 10).
  • 10 vs 10 or ace: hit (same as American).
  • A,A vs ace: hit instead of splitting.
  • 8,8 vs 10 or ace: hit instead of splitting.

FAQ

Which version do European casinos deal?

Most often the no-hole-card version with 6 decks. Exact rules vary: check the table sign.

Should you double 11 vs 10 with no hole card?

No, just hit: a dealer blackjack would take the doubled bet.

Does card counting work in European blackjack?

Yes, the principle is the same. Only a few deviation indexes change.
